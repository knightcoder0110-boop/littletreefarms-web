Do You Own Rural Land You No Longer Want?

Perhaps you inherited a woodlot, own vacant land your family no longer uses, or have reached a point where maintaining the property has become difficult. You may still care deeply about what happens to it.

Little Tree Farm is developing a long-term land stewardship program for people who want their rural property to continue supporting forests, wildlife, biodiversity, seed production and future generations. Every property is different, and there is no single solution — we welcome a conversation with landowners who would like to explore what their land could become.

Land Should Continue to Have Purpose

Land can provide value far beyond its sale price.

A woodland can produce native seed for future forests. Abandoned farmland can become a nut orchard, wildlife habitat or restored forest. Wetlands can continue storing water, supporting wildlife and protecting biodiversity.

Some properties may remain largely natural. Others may be actively restored, planted or managed over many decades. The goal here isn't development — it's giving suitable rural land a thoughtful, productive and lasting future.

Possible Futures

What Could the Land Become?

Every property is different. Depending on soil, access, existing habitat and your own wishes, a piece of land might take on any of the following roles.

Native Forest Restoration

Restoring degraded land with native hardwoods, shrubs and forest communities.

Wildlife Habitat

Improving nesting areas, shelter, food sources, corridors and habitat diversity.

Native Seed Orchards

A reliable source of locally adapted seed for future restoration and nursery production.

Nut Orchards

Planting walnut, chestnut, hazelnut, hickory and other long-lived food or timber species.

Nursery Production

Using appropriate open land to grow bare-root trees and shrubs for future planting projects.

Wetland Protection

Allowing wetlands, riparian areas and watercourses to remain protected and functional.

Pollinator Habitat

Flowering shrubs and native plants supporting insects and birds through the seasons.

Educational Forests

A demonstration property where people learn about trees, seed collection and woodland care.

Long-Term Managed Forest

Maintaining or improving forest cover through patient, site-appropriate management.

Not every use is suitable for every property. Soil, access, existing habitat, location, size, zoning and your own wishes will all shape what's possible.

A Legacy, Not Just a Transaction

Leave a Living Legacy

"A property can represent decades of family history, work and memories. For many landowners, the hardest question isn't how much the land is worth — it's what will happen to it next."

Will it remain forested? Will it keep supporting wildlife? Could it grow trees, or seed, for generations still to come? A land legacy lets the values connected to a property outlast a change in ownership — we want to help create places that stay useful, alive and cared for over the next 50, 100, or even 200 years.

Scope

What We're Looking For

The program is mainly focused on rural and environmentally useful properties. A property doesn't need to be perfect — but we also can't promise to accept every property submitted.

Potentially Suitable Properties

  • Forested land & woodlots
  • Wetlands
  • Abandoned agricultural land or old pasture
  • Rural acreage with restoration potential
  • Land suitable for seed orchards or tree planting
  • Land containing important habitat
  • Mixed woodland and open land

Factors We May Consider

  • Location, acreage & road access
  • Property taxes & zoning
  • Existing buildings, mortgages or liens
  • Ecological value, soil & drainage
  • Existing forest cover & restoration potential
  • Long-term management costs
